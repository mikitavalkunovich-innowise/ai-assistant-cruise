# CDC Vessel Sanitation Program (VSP) — Galley Operations Requirements

**Source:** CDC VSP Operations Manual (public guidance)  
**Applies to:** Cruise ships operating in US waters

## Overview

The CDC Vessel Sanitation Program conducts unannounced operational inspections of cruise ships carrying 13 or more passengers with international itineraries. Galley operations are a primary inspection focus area.

## Galley Inspection Requirements

### Temperature Control
- Hot foods must be maintained at **135°F (57°C) or above**
- Cold foods must be maintained at **41°F (5°C) or below**
- Temperature logs must be maintained for all hot and cold holding units
- Thermometers must be calibrated daily and records kept for 30 days

### Food Handling
- All food handlers must complete food safety training within 30 days of employment
- Bare hand contact with ready-to-eat food is prohibited — use utensils, deli tissue, or gloves
- Handwashing required: before food preparation, after handling raw meat, after using restroom, after touching face or hair
- Handwashing stations must have: warm running water, soap, single-use towels, waste container

### Cross-Contamination Prevention
- Separate cutting boards and utensils for raw and cooked foods (color-coded system recommended)
- Raw meat, poultry, and seafood must be stored below ready-to-eat foods in refrigeration
- All food must be covered or wrapped during storage
- Chemical sanitizing solution: chlorine 50-100 ppm or quaternary ammonium 200 ppm

### Galley Cleanliness
- All food contact surfaces must be cleaned and sanitized every 4 hours during operation
- Non-food contact surfaces cleaned at least daily
- Floor drains must be clean and free of debris
- Grease traps cleaned per manufacturer schedule (minimum monthly)
- Pest control measures documented and current

### Documentation Required
- Food temperature logs (hot holding, cold holding, cooking temperatures)
- Employee illness reporting log
- Cleaning and sanitizing schedules
- Pest control service records
- Water quality test results (if applicable)

## Inspection Scoring

VSP inspections use a 100-point scale:
- **86–100:** Satisfactory
- **71–85:** Not satisfactory — corrective action required
- **≤70:** Not satisfactory — re-inspection required; may result in operational restrictions

Galley-related violations carry significant point deductions:
- Improper food temperatures: 4–8 points per violation
- Cross-contamination risk: 4–6 points
- Poor personal hygiene: 2–4 points per employee
- Inadequate handwashing facilities: 4 points

## Crew Certification

All galley staff must hold a valid **USPH Sanitation Certificate**, renewed every 2 years. Training must cover:
- HACCP principles
- Personal hygiene requirements
- Temperature control procedures
- Cleaning and sanitizing protocols
- Illness reporting requirements

## Reporting

Report suspected foodborne illness outbreaks to the VSP immediately: vsp@cdc.gov or 1-800-CDC-INFO.

Ship medical staff must maintain gastrointestinal illness logs per VSP requirements (reporting threshold: 2% of passengers or 2% of crew with GI symptoms in a single voyage).
